# Scopes

When an app asks a person to sign in, it also says what it wants to know: their name, their email address, the roles they hold. Each of those is requested by name. The name is a scope, and it is the vocabulary an app and a person use to agree on what is shared.

_In words: A client asks for scopes. They are sorted against the default and optional scopes the client has, a consent screen may let the person choose among the optional ones, and what was allowed decides the claims in the access token, the ID token and the UserInfo answer._

## What a scope does

A scope is a name with three jobs.

1. **It says what the client wants.** The client sends `scope=openid email`, and Odudu reads each word.
2. **It adds claims.** `email` adds the person's address. `profile` adds their name. `roles` and `groups` add what they hold. Each scope says whether its claims go in the ID token, the access token or both, with `include_in_id_token` and `include_in_access_token`.
3. **It is what a person approves.** On the consent screen each scope is shown by its `consent_text`, in the order you give it with `display_order`.

`openid` is the scope that turns a request into a sign-in. Without it the client gets no ID token.

## Default and optional

A tenant defines its scopes. A client does not get all of them. Each client is assigned the ones it may ask for, and each assignment is either **default** or **optional**:

- A **default** scope is given whenever the client asks. On the consent screen it is listed, but the person cannot untick it, because an app without `openid` cannot work at all.
- An **optional** scope is the person's choice. It shows a tick box. Next time, the box remembers what they allowed.

A scope can also be marked to be given to every new client, as default or as optional, through `default_client_assignment`.

A client that asks for a scope it was not assigned is refused with `invalid_scope`.

## Roles reach a token through a scope

Roles are not claims on their own. You map a role to a scope, and the role appears in a token only when that scope was granted. A person with the `reviewer` role who signs in without the `roles` scope gets a token that does not mention it. [Roles and groups](./roles-and-groups.mdx) explains how a person comes to hold a role.

This is also why the ID token and the access token can differ. The `roles` and `groups` scopes ship with `include_in_id_token` off: an ID token travels through the browser, and a full list of roles has no business there.

## What UserInfo does with a request

An app can name the claims it wants from the UserInfo endpoint in a `claims` request. Odudu then returns exactly those, plus `sub`, even when the granted scope would have allowed more. A client that says what it needs gets only that. Details are in the [UserInfo endpoint reference](../reference/generated/endpoints.md).

## Working with scopes

1. List the claims your app really needs, and nothing else.
2. Find or create a scope for each. The [scopes operations](../reference/generated/admin-api/scopes.md) create, change and delete them.
3. Assign each to the client as default if the app cannot work without it, and as optional if the person may refuse.
4. Give optional and sensitive scopes a clear `consent_text`, written for the person who reads it.
5. Request them when you start a sign-in, as in [Sign a user in](../guides/sign-a-user-in.md).
