# Branding in the console

A tenant's users meet Odudu's pages when they sign in, consent, reset a password or set up a second factor. **Branding** is where the tenant makes those pages its own. It needs the `manage-tenant` capability; the menu entry is **Branding** under **TENANT**.

Nothing you change is seen until you publish it. Edits are saved as a draft, the preview shows the draft drawn by the real page, and **Publish** makes it what people see.

## 1. Set the colours and images

On the **Theme** tab, **Colours and font** holds a light palette and a dark palette. Each is used whole: turn on **Use my own light colours** and every colour in that scheme is yours, starting from Instrument's own. A scheme you leave alone keeps Instrument's colours. Under **Images**, upload a logo (with the alt text it needs), a logo for the dark scheme, a favicon and a background image. PNG, JPEG and WebP are accepted, and each slot states its size limit beside it.

Each palette has eleven colours: Page background, Panel background, Field background, Text, Muted text, Accent background, Accent text, Error background, Error text, Border and Focus ring. Type each as `#rrggbb` or pick it. The Font is Instrument’s own, IBM Plex Sans or IBM Plex Mono, and is set for both schemes at once.

The **Contrast** panel checks every pair of colours as you type. Text must reach 4.5:1 against what it sits on, and borders and the focus ring 3:1. **Publish** is held while a pair falls short, and the panel names it.

## 2. Change a page's layout

On the **Pages** tab, choose a page and edit its template. The parts the server fills in, such as the form and its fields, are listed beside the editor; a template places them and may not add scripts, styles or forms. A refused template says what is wrong and where.

## 3. Preview, publish, restore

The preview beside the editor frames the real page for the saved draft, in the light or the dark scheme. **Publish** makes the saved draft the published theme and keeps the version it replaces, up to twenty. **Versions** lists them; **Restore** publishes a copy of an earlier one and leaves the draft alone.

## 4. See the mail

The **Mail** tab shows the address-verification mail as the published theme dresses it, in the light or the dark scheme and in a language you choose, drawn by the code that sends it. Beside it, **What the mail inherits** lists the logo and the nine colours of each scheme (Page background, **Message background**, **Link box background**, Border, Text, **Quiet text**, Link, Button and **Button text**) and says whether the tenant's published theme or Odudu's default supplies each palette. Mail follows the tenant's theme only; a client's own theme never reaches it, and a draft does not show until it is published.

## 5. Choose the languages

On the **Languages** tab, **Supported languages** is the list of languages the tenant's pages and mail are offered in, and **Default language** is the one used when a visitor's browser does not decide. Choose "The browser decides" to clear the default. To offer another language, type its tag or pick it under Add a language and save; its pages use the English text unless a message override says otherwise. Under **Message overrides**, pick a language and set the text a page shows for a message key such as `login.title`; a key the catalogue does not hold is refused, and a tenant holds at most 500 overrides across every language. Edit an override by choosing **Edit** on its row, and remove it with **Remove**. Overrides change pages; mail is not changed by them.

## Things that cannot be undone

**Reset to default** deletes the draft and unpublishes the theme, so people see Instrument's own pages again. The version that was published stays under **Versions**. **Delete image** removes a stored image, and is refused while any theme still names it.

## Do the same through the API

The calls are in the [theme](../reference/generated/admin-api/theme/index.md) area of the admin API: reading and saving the draft, publishing, versions and restore, reset, image upload and delete, and the preview. The mail preview and what the mail inherits are `GET /mail/preview` and `GET /mail/branding`; the overrides are the `/message-overrides` routes.
